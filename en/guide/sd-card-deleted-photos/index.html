Deleted photos from an SD card? 3 things not to do

The first few minutes matter most. Written from hands-on recovery work.

What hurts your chances isn't the delete. It's what you do after it. Avoid these three things and you keep the card as it is right now.

Why deleted files can still be there

Think of a card as a library. Deleting a file doesn't burn the book. The computer only removes that line from the catalog at the entrance and marks the shelf as free for new books.

That is why recovery can work. It is also why the original is really gone once a new book takes that shelf. All three mistakes below put new books on the shelf.

1. Don't keep using the card

This is the most common and most costly mistake. New files can land exactly where the deleted ones were. That includes copying a file over for a moment, or putting the card back in your camera or dashcam.

Do this now: Safely remove the card and leave it out.

2. Don't download recovery software onto that card

Installing a program on the card writes over the space you want to save. Saving found files back to the same card causes the same problem.

Do this now: Install the program on your PC and save recovered files to a different drive.

3. Don't keep formatting or running error checks

If Windows asks you to format the drive or scan it for errors, press Cancel. Repeated formatting can clear remaining traces, and an error check tidies a drive for reuse rather than rescuing your files.

Do this now: Cancel the prompt and remove the card.

What to do next

When recovery is unlikely

  • Many new files were saved after the delete
  • The card was formatted repeatedly
  • Your PC doesn't detect the card at all
  • The card has water damage or physical damage
